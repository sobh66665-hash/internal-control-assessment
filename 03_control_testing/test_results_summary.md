# Control Testing Results Summary

## 1. Assessment Overview

The Procure-to-Pay control testing program evaluated 10 key controls covering procurement, vendor management, receiving, accounts payable, payment authorization, user access, and management monitoring.

The testing was designed to determine whether controls operated consistently and whether identified exceptions indicated weaknesses requiring management attention.

---

## 2. Overall Results

| Control Status | Number of Controls |
|---|---:|
| Effective | 4 |
| Partially Effective | 4 |
| Ineffective | 2 |
| **Total** | **10** |

### Overall Assessment

The testing results indicate that the control environment is **partially effective**, with several controls operating as intended but material weaknesses identified in vendor management, segregation of duties, procurement authorization, invoice exception management, and monitoring.

---

## 3. Key Testing Results

### C-01 — Purchase Requisition Approval

**Result:** Effective

12 of 12 sampled requisitions had appropriate approval within delegated authority.

**Conclusion:** No exceptions identified.

---

### C-02 — Approved Requisition Before PO

**Result:** Partially Effective

11 of 12 purchase orders were supported by prior approved requisitions.

**Exception:** 1 purchase order was issued before documented requisition approval.

**Related Finding:** IC-03

---

### C-03 — Independent Vendor Approval

**Result:** Ineffective

7 of 10 sampled vendors had documented independent Finance approval.

**Exception:** 3 vendors lacked sufficient evidence of independent approval.

**Related Finding:** IC-01

---

### C-04 — Supplier Bank Detail Changes

**Result:** Effective

8 of 8 sampled bank-detail changes had documented verification and approval.

**Conclusion:** No exceptions identified.

---

### C-05 — Goods Receipt Before Payment

**Result:** Effective

12 of 12 sampled invoices were supported by receiving evidence before payment eligibility.

**Conclusion:** No exceptions identified.

---

### C-06 — Three-Way Matching

**Result:** Partially Effective

13 of 15 sampled invoices matched without exception.

**Exception:** 2 invoices contained unresolved quantity differences.

**Related Finding:** IC-04

---

### C-07 — Payment Authorization

**Result:** Effective

10 of 10 sampled payment batches were approved within documented authority limits.

**Conclusion:** No exceptions identified.

---

### C-08 — Segregation of Duties / User Access

**Result:** Ineffective

Two incompatible access combinations were identified.

**Risk:** Users may have the ability to perform incompatible procurement and Accounts Payable activities.

**Related Finding:** IC-02

---

### C-09 — Monthly Exception Monitoring

**Result:** Partially Effective

2 of 3 monthly reports contained complete documented follow-up.

**Exception:** One report lacked documented follow-up for overdue exceptions.

**Related Finding:** IC-05

---

### C-10 — Supporting Documentation

**Result:** Partially Effective

18 of 20 sampled transactions had complete supporting documentation.

**Exception:** 2 transactions lacked complete supporting evidence.

**Management Attention:** Documentation retention and completeness should be strengthened.

---

# 4. Key Themes

The testing identified five primary control themes:

1. **Vendor Master Governance**
2. **Segregation of Duties**
3. **Procurement Authorization**
4. **Invoice Exception Management**
5. **Management Monitoring**

These themes were carried forward into the internal control findings and improvement plan.

---

# 5. Audit Conclusion

Based on the fictional sample tested, the Procure-to-Pay control environment demonstrates a reasonable foundational structure but requires targeted improvements.

The most significant weaknesses relate to controls that could affect:

- Unauthorized transactions
- Fraud exposure
- Payment accuracy
- System access
- Management oversight

Management remediation should therefore prioritize the high-risk findings while strengthening monitoring and evidence retention across the process.
