# Management Improvement Plan

## 1. Objective

The purpose of this improvement plan is to translate internal control findings into practical remediation actions with clear ownership, priorities, target dates, and measurable outcomes.

The plan is designed to address the identified control weaknesses while improving the overall effectiveness of the Procure-to-Pay control environment.

## 3. Recommended Control Enhancements

### 3.1 Vendor Master Controls

Management should implement:

* Mandatory vendor onboarding documentation.
* Independent Finance approval.
* Separation between vendor creation and approval.
* Periodic duplicate-vendor review.
* Independent verification of bank-account changes.
* Audit trail for all vendor master changes.

### 3.2 Segregation of Duties

Establish a formal segregation-of-duties matrix covering:

* Procurement
* Vendor creation
* Goods receipt
* Accounts Payable
* Payment preparation
* Payment approval

Incompatible combinations should be blocked whenever technically possible.

Any approved exception should be documented, risk-assessed, and independently monitored.

### 3.3 Procurement Workflow

The procurement workflow should enforce:

```text
Approved Requisition
        ↓
PO Creation Enabled
```

An approved purchase requisition should be a mandatory prerequisite for purchase-order creation.

### 3.4 Invoice Controls

Management should strengthen invoice controls through:

* Automated three-way matching.
* Defined tolerance thresholds.
* Exception workflow.
* Documented resolution of discrepancies.
* Payment blocking for unresolved material exceptions.
* Periodic review of recurring invoice exceptions.

### 3.5 User Access Management

The organization should implement:

* Role-based access.
* Formal access approval.
* Documented access-change requests.
* Quarterly user access reviews.
* Segregation-of-duties conflict monitoring.
* Timely removal of terminated-user access.

### 3.6 Exception Monitoring

Every significant control exception should have:

**Owner → Due Date → Status → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Closure Evidence**

High-risk overdue exceptions should be escalated to senior management.

# 5. Follow-Up Approach

Internal Audit should perform a remediation follow-up after the agreed target dates.

The follow-up should:

1. Obtain evidence supporting each corrective action.
2. Validate that the action has actually been implemented.
3. Re-test the affected control where appropriate.
4. Confirm whether the identified risk has been reduced.
5. Evaluate any remaining residual risk.
6. Determine whether the finding can be closed.
7. Document management's outstanding actions where remediation is incomplete.

---

# 6. Expected Business Benefits

Successful implementation should improve:

### Financial Protection

Reduce the risk of unauthorized, duplicate, or inaccurate payments.

### Fraud Prevention

Strengthen controls against fictitious suppliers and inappropriate transactions.

### Process Efficiency

Reduce recurring exceptions and manual investigation.

### Governance

Create clearer accountability for control ownership and remediation.

### Auditability

Improve the availability and quality of supporting documentation.

### Management Visibility

Provide measurable indicators of control performance.

### Risk Management

Move from reactive issue identification toward proactive risk and control monitoring.

---

# 7. Management Success Criteria

The remediation program should be considered effective when:

* High-risk control gaps have been addressed.
* Identified SoD conflicts have been resolved or formally approved.
* Vendor approvals are consistently documented.
* Purchase orders cannot bypass required approval.
* Material invoice exceptions are resolved before payment.
* Control exceptions are assigned and monitored.
* Management receives regular control-performance reporting.

---

## Final Perspective

Remediation should not be viewed simply as closing audit findings.

The objective is to strengthen the underlying process so that the same risk is less likely to recur.

**Effective remediation = Risk Reduction + Sustainable Control Improvement + Measurable Monitoring**
