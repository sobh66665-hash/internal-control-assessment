
# Process Overview — Procure-to-Pay (P2P)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured overview of the Procure-to-Pay (P2P) process for Alpha Trading & Distribution Co.

The purpose is to understand how procurement transactions flow from the initial purchase request through supplier payment, identify key control points, and establish a foundation for risk assessment and control testing.

The assessment focuses on the relationship between:

**Process → Risk → Control → Testing → Finding → Recommendation**

---

## 2. Business Scenario

Alpha Trading & Distribution Co. is a fictional mid-sized trading and distribution company operating through multiple regional branches.

The company purchases inventory, office supplies, maintenance services, logistics services, and other operational requirements from approximately 180 active suppliers.

The P2P process involves multiple departments and roles, creating potential risks related to authorization, supplier integrity, segregation of duties, system access, payment accuracy, and management monitoring.

All company names, employees, suppliers, transactions, samples, findings, and scenarios used in this repository are fictional and created for demonstration purposes.

---

## 3. Process Flow

The standard Procure-to-Pay process is designed to follow these sequential stages:

**Purchase Requisition**
↓
**Management Approval**
↓
**Purchase Order**
↓
**Goods / Service Receipt**
↓
**Supplier Invoice**
↓
**Three-Way Match**
↓
**Payment Proposal**
↓
**Payment Approval**
↓
**Supplier Payment**

Each stage represents a potential control point where financial, operational, compliance, or fraud-related risks may arise.

---

## 4. Key Process Roles

### Requester

Initiates the purchase requisition and provides the business justification and required specifications.

### Department Manager

Reviews and approves purchase requisitions according to delegated authority limits.

### Procurement Officer

Obtains quotations where applicable, selects suppliers according to procurement procedures, and issues purchase orders after required approvals.

### Receiving Officer

Confirms that goods or services have been received and records the receipt in the system.

### Accounts Payable Accountant

Processes supplier invoices, performs the required matching procedures, and prepares payment proposals.

### Finance Manager

Reviews financial transactions, approves supplier payments within delegated authority, and oversees key financial controls.

### CFO

Provides senior financial oversight and approves transactions exceeding delegated authority limits.

### IT / System Administrator

Manages user access, system roles, and access rights in accordance with approved job responsibilities.

---

## 5. Key Control Principles

The P2P process should be supported by the following fundamental control principles:

### Authorization

Transactions should be approved by authorized individuals before financial commitments are made.

### Segregation of Duties

Incompatible responsibilities should not be assigned to the same individual, particularly across procurement, receiving, accounts payable, and payment activities.

### Vendor Master Integrity

Supplier creation and changes to supplier information should be subject to independent verification and approval.

### Three-Way Matching

Supplier invoices should be matched against the purchase order and goods/service receipt before payment.

### Payment Authorization

Supplier payments should be reviewed and approved according to documented authority limits.

### User Access Management

System access should be aligned with job responsibilities and periodically reviewed.

### Management Monitoring

Control exceptions should be documented, assigned to responsible owners, monitored, and escalated when overdue.

---

## 6. Key Risks

The major risks considered within the P2P process include:

* Unauthorized or unnecessary purchases
* Purchase commitments made without appropriate approval
* Fictitious or unauthorized suppliers
* Fraudulent changes to supplier bank details
* Payment for goods or services not received
* Duplicate or inaccurate supplier payments
* Unauthorized cash outflows
* Incompatible user access
* Inadequate segregation of duties
* Unresolved control exceptions
* Incomplete supporting documentation
* Insufficient audit evidence

## 8. Assessment Perspective

The assessment does not focus only on whether individual transactions were processed correctly.

It evaluates whether the overall process is designed and operating in a way that:

* Prevents or detects significant risks
* Protects company assets
* Supports reliable financial information
* Reduces opportunities for fraud
* Maintains appropriate accountability
* Provides sufficient audit evidence
* Enables management to monitor and resolve exceptions

This approach connects financial information with the underlying business processes and control environment.
